Before moving to the University of Bern, where he teaches North American Literature and Culture, Axel Stähler was Professor of Comparative Literature at the University of Kent at Canterbury. He is a Leverhulme Fellow and currently PI of the research project Jewish Writing in the Global South, funded by the Swiss National Science Foundation, and a member of a DFG-network on Jewish Multilingualism. His research interests include modern Jewish writing, literature and fundamentalism, and intermediality. He has published widely on anglophone Jewish literature, British Jewish writing, the Holocaust, and on the convergence of Zionist, racial, and colonial discourses in early twentieth-century German Jewish literature and culture. His latest book, about the destruction of Jerusalem in nineteenth-century German culture, was published in 2025. He has just completed the manusript for a book about representations of the blood libel on the stage and the silver screen.
