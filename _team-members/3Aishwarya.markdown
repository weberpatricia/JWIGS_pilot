<p style="text-align: justify;">
Aishwarya Khale holds a Master’s degree (Honours) in English Literature from the University of Mumbai. She also studied Creative Writing at Exeter College, University of Oxford, and held a research affiliation with the Department of Comparative Literature at Harvard University (Cambridge, Massachusetts). Her doctoral thesis is tentatively titled “Corporeal Cartographies: Toward a Comparative Phenomenology of Dalit and Jewish Bodies.”
</p>
<p style="text-align: justify;">
Her work has been published by the Royal Society of Literature (UK), Barnes & Noble Digital Press, MQAM’s critics’ review section on IMDb International, The Criterion: An International Journal in English, The Round Table Literary Journal (USA), the Staffordshire History Festival, The Wire, the Hudson Valley Writers Guild (USA), the Appalachia Studio Blog, Melbourne Culture Corner, Wokingham Today, The London Reader, the Hillingdon Women’s Archive, Creative Ireland, Muse India, and the Cork Festival Poetry Initiative. Her interviews and creative work have been broadcast on BBC Radio (London), CIUT Radio (Toronto), and The Telescope Podcast (Apple Podcasts), among other literary and media platforms. She briefly served as a Review Editor for Meridians, a journal published by Smith College (Massachusetts, USA), and has hosted an episode of Rethinking the Global, a podcast series produced by the Centre for Global and Transregional Studies (Bern).
</p>
<p style="text-align: justify;">
She has presented her research at, and participated in, numerous international conferences and workshops, including those hosted by the HRF Network (Germany), Harvard University (USA), the University of Tokyo (Japan), the Theatre and Drama Network (Istanbul), Mazovian State University (Poland), Johns Hopkins University (USA), and the Symbiosis Law Institute (Bangalore). She is a member of the Royal Asiatic Society (UK), the European Association for South Asian Studies (Germany), the South Asian Literary Association (USA), and the New Voices in Postcolonial Studies Network (UK).
</p>
